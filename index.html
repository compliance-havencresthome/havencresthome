What our DSPs do not do

Clear boundaries protect you and keep us honest. Haven Crest Homes provides non-medical support only.

  • No nursing or medical care, wound care, injections, or clinical assessments
  • No medication administration beyond reminders and assistance allowed by your program and plan
  • No overnight awake staffing unless specifically authorized and staffed
  • No financial management, no handling of your money or accounts

Our DSPs observe and report. If something changes, your care manager, family or physician hears about it promptly.

How it works

From first call to a confirmed plan.

A conversation

Call or send a request. We ask what a typical day looks like, what has become difficult, and who else is on your care team. Every inquiry gets a response within one business day.

A home visit and a written plan

We meet you at home, learn your routines and preferences, and write a Service Plan with you. If you have a care manager or IRIS consultant, we coordinate with them on authorized hours.

Consistent support

You're matched with a DSP trained on your plan. A supervisor checks in regularly, and you and your family always know who to call.

Programs and coverage

Most of our members don't pay out of pocket.

In-home support like ours is covered for many Wisconsin residents through Medicaid long-term care programs. Not sure where you stand? Call us and we'll walk through it together.

Family Care Medicaid

Family Care is Wisconsin's managed long-term care program for frail elders and adults with disabilities. A care manager at your managed care organization (MCO) authorizes the services in your plan. Ask them to authorize Haven Crest Homes.

I have Medicare or a Medicare Advantage plan. Am I covered?

Generally no. Medicare and Medicare Advantage plans do not cover ongoing non-medical in-home support. If that's the only coverage you have, call us anyway. Many people qualify for Medicaid long-term care without knowing it, and we'll point you to the right office.

Can a family member be paid to provide my care?

In some situations, yes. Family Care and IRIS allow certain family members to be hired as paid caregivers through an agency, subject to program rules and MCO approval. Haven Crest Homes can employ, train and supervise a family caregiver so the support stays reliable and compliant. Ask your care manager, then ask us.

For families

You don't have to figure this out alone.

Most families call us at a hard moment: a fall, a hospital discharge, a parent who insists they're fine. Here's what we tell them.

How do I know it's time for in-home support?

Common signs: missed meals or medications, a decline in hygiene, unopened mail, a recent fall, a caregiver who is exhausted, or a doctor or discharge planner recommending help at home. Support can start small, a few mornings a week, and grow only if needed. Our goal is to keep someone independent, not to take over.

What does the first visit look like?

A supervisor comes to the home for about an hour. We walk through a typical day, look at the home together for safety, talk about preferences (how coffee is taken matters), and write a Service Plan. Family is welcome in the conversation when the member wants them there.

Will it be the same person every time?

That's the goal, and it's why we invest in keeping our DSPs. You'll have a primary DSP trained on the plan and a named backup who has also met the member, so a sick day doesn't mean a stranger at the door.

How will I know how things are going?

Every visit is documented. With the member's permission, we share updates with family on the schedule you prefer: a quick text after visits, a weekly summary, or a call when something changes. If a DSP notices a change in health, mobility or mood, you and the care team hear about it the same day.

My parent is in the hospital or rehab and coming home soon.

Call us before discharge, not after. We can meet the discharge planner's timeline, set up the home, and provide extra hours during the first two weeks when the risk of a return trip is highest. Ask the discharge planner to send us a referral; the checklist above tells them what we need.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers.

Are your caregivers employees or contractors?

Employees. Every DSP passes a Wisconsin caregiver background check before hire, completes competency-based training before the first visit, and is supervised by a Haven Crest Homes supervisor. We carry the insurance and the responsibility.

Is there a minimum number of hours?

For program members, we follow the hours authorized in your plan. For private-pay families, visits are typically a minimum of two hours so a DSP has time to do the work well. We'll be upfront if a schedule isn't one we can staff reliably.

What happens if my DSP is sick?

You're told as early as possible and offered the named backup who has already met you. We track missed and late visits as a core quality measure because reliability is the whole point.

How quickly can services start?

For program members, usually within a week of authorization. For private pay, often within a few days of the home visit. Hospital discharges can move faster when the discharge planner contacts us early.

Do you serve adults with developmental disabilities?

Yes. Much of our work is with adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities who want to live in their own homes with support that respects their choices and builds skills rather than dependence.

Are you hiring?

We're always looking for dependable, compassionate DSPs in Douglas and St. Croix counties, including family caregivers who want to be employed, trained and supported. Email compliance@havencresthome.com with "DSP" in the subject line.
